Bloom Trading bot drain

Wiki id bloom-trading-bot-drain-oct-2026 · Live incident page

Summary

On 26 Oct 2026, Bloom Trading’s Telegram trading bot wallets sent 9,408.229795593 SOL to a brand-new attacker 7kY8qx8MqJ7Rz2vMhgHETdZLHcTp1oM78AA9dmE9p8BQ in 59 successful System transfers from 48 wallets belonging to 9 users. Per-user 0.001 SOL probes started at 2026-10-26T01:00:28Z; the main sweep ran 2026-10-26T01:03:07Z–01:04:41Z. Six address-poisoning dust transfers (0.000132 SOL total) are excluded. Bloom refunded 9,408.2297 SOL the same day. Stolen SOL later fanned out across 237+ single-use addresses; the endpoint is unresolved.

Impact

59 real victim transfers from 48 wallets (9 users): 9,408.229795593 SOL. Public “65 transfers from 53 addresses” counts include 6 address-poisoning dust txs (0.000132 SOL) from lookalike vanity addresses; those are not victims. Bloom claimed 49 wallets; 48 are on chain. Only 0.000001 USDC reached the attacker ATA 59Hb4BQ5mFrgDnY4rKixJfQu61mUNcSkmvkgPfqdNTRZ. Same-day refunds of 9,408.2297 SOL (9,399.2297 SOL refunds + 9 SOL tests) match the drained total to 0.0001 SOL. Recipients are not the drained wallets. This entry does not convert SOL to USD.

On-chain evidence

Every drain is a legacy tx with one signature: the victim wallet is signer and fee payer. Instructions are ComputeBudget::SetComputeUnitLimit(100000), ComputeBudget::SetComputeUnitPrice(1000000), and system::transfer to the attacker. No Bloom program, delegate, PDA, or session authority is on the drain txs. Victims paid 105,000 lamports each. The attacker was never pre-funded.

Root cause (reported)

Reported by Bloom: RCE in a third-party module. That claim is not independently verified here.

On-chain, the drains are plain System transfers signed and fee-paid by the victim wallets themselves. That is consistent with the bot being made to sign server-side. It is equally consistent with the keys being read out of the server. Chain cannot tell a key exfiltration apart from abuse of an in-process signer and does not prove that keys were or were not compromised. Per-user probing shows the attacker had the user↔wallet mapping.

Lessons

Custodial / server-held bot keys make a server compromise indistinguishable from user signatures. Bots need per-transaction policy limits (destination allowlists, spend caps, delays) so a compromised app server cannot sweep.
