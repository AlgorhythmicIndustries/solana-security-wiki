Binance hot-wallet drain

Wiki id binance-hot-wallet-drain-oct-2026 · Live incident page

Summary

On 29 Oct 2026 the Solana hot wallet 5tzFkiKscXHK5ZXCGbXZxdw7gTjjD1mBwuoFbhUvuAi9 (“Binance 2” label; attribution only) sent 970,521.9 SOL (~$95.8M at drain-time price, DefiLlama) to attacker AffzFDKZbyMiU11zmVBoAqD25ugpr7xoWWyRyGigBEaU in 5 transfers from 2026-10-29T20:10:18Z to 2026-10-29T20:33:29Z. No SPL tokens were taken. The 5 drain txs are bare legacy System transfers. The wallet’s 1,125 normal withdrawals in the same window used a durable nonce plus compute-budget instructions, so the drain did not come through Binance’s normal withdrawal pipeline. As of 2026-11-01T02:04:53Z the attacker still held 970,554.16 SOL and had signed no transactions. Starting 2026-11-01T09:38:28Z, AffzFDKZbyMiU11zmVBoAqD25ugpr7xoWWyRyGigBEaU signed its first outbound txs. The 7 SOL test and 485,270 SOL went to fresh wallet Eze86Ngj2Ue4xKBbTHgXbhm22xnVF4aTxKrhUuVNDrrD (unmoved). 5,883.78 SOL went to hop wallet 7SDuVQ2SkaHBURbxzyXAr8coNcLV2ZuSLkteoRYfQ2fK. As of ~2026-11-01T11:12Z: AffzFD 479,393.39 SOL, Eze86 485,277.01 SOL, 7SDu 4,544.19 SOL (still active), 1.01958279 BTC at bc1q8xnarcqhjkg3v77dtzy4x9vrqst9e7z4n04ps5, and 0.60722605 BTC at bc1q4xj8j5d5dj3lhk45asqnyf36s9q8azx7rraje7. Lookalike-address dust sent to the hop wallets is third-party address poisoning, not attacker flow. The X claim of “486,970 SOL swapped to BTC via Relay” is not supported on chain. Reported claims that this wallet “sent tokens” to memecoin dev wallets ($NOTSAFU, $LAZARUS, $TT) are false.

Context

This is the Solana side of a multi-chain incident of about $6.3B (Binance’s figure). Per-chain on-chain split: EVM about $3.82B, BTC about $1.47B, Tron about $0.63B, XRP about $0.30B, SOL about $0.10B. Other chains are covered in the living report.

Reported by Binance: a compromised DevOps engineer’s laptop led to a service container that submitted unauthorized withdrawals, with “no evidence of any wallet private key leak”. Later updates spoke of “multiple factors” and “additional points of breach”. Those claims are not independently verified here.

On Ethereum, Binance 14’s own key signed the EIP-7702 authorizations. The chain cannot prove no key leaked.

Attribution: On 2026-10-31 the FBI, together with USSS and IRS-CI, attributed the theft of more than $6B from Binance (on or about 2026-10-29) to the Democratic People’s Republic of Korea (North Korea). The FBI names this activity “TraderTraitor”. The FBI’s published address list includes exactly one Solana address, AffzFDKZbyMiU11zmVBoAqD25ugpr7xoWWyRyGigBEaU, the Solana drain recipient (alongside 608 ETH and 19 BTC addresses). Earlier TRM and Chainalysis claims attributing the incident to DPRK with high confidence are now consistent with the FBI attribution.

Impact

970,521.9 SOL in 5 System transfers (~$95.8M at drain-time price, DefiLlama). No SPL tokens were taken; token outflows in the window have the normal withdrawal shape. Public figures that cite 965,455 SOL are the first tx alone; 970,500 is rounded. As of 2026-11-01T02:04:53Z the attacker held 970,554.16 SOL and had signed no transactions (prior snapshot). As of ~2026-11-01T11:12Z: AffzFD 479,393.39 SOL, Eze86 485,277.01 SOL (unmoved), 7SDu 4,544.19 SOL (still active), 1.01958279 BTC at bc1q8xnarcqhjkg3v77dtzy4x9vrqst9e7z4n04ps5, and 0.60722605 BTC at bc1q4xj8j5d5dj3lhk45asqnyf36s9q8azx7rraje7. 1,338.157475 SOL became 1.62681826 BTC via Relay. Nothing has been frozen on Solana.

On-chain evidence

Every drain is a legacy tx signed solely by 5tzFkiKscXHK5ZXCGbXZxdw7gTjjD1mBwuoFbhUvuAi9, which is also the fee payer. Instruction is one bare system::transfer. Recent blockhash, no durable nonce, no ComputeBudget instructions, 5,000-lamport fee.

In the window 19:30–22:00Z the source had 1,615 txs and 513 SOL-outflow txs. All 1,125 normal outflows use advanceNonce (durable nonce, authority 5tzFki…) plus 2 ComputeBudget instructions plus transfer(s). The 5 drains are the only outflows with a different shape. So they were signed by the hot-wallet key but built outside Binance’s normal withdrawal tx builder.

Root cause (reported)

Reported by Binance: a compromised DevOps engineer’s laptop led to a service container that submitted unauthorized withdrawals, with “no evidence of any wallet private key leak”. Later updates spoke of “multiple factors” and “additional points of breach”. Those claims are not independently verified here.

On-chain, the 5 drains were signed by the hot-wallet key but built outside Binance’s normal durable-nonce withdrawal pipeline. That is consistent with a compromised signing service being handed raw txs. It is equally consistent with direct key use. Chain cannot tell the two apart and does not prove that keys were or were not leaked, so “no key leak” stays Binance’s claim. On Ethereum, Binance 14’s own key signed the EIP-7702 authorizations; that also cannot prove the key did not leak.

Lessons

A hot-wallet signer that will sign a bare System transfer is a withdrawal-pipeline bypass. Policy has to sit at the signer (shape, destination, amount), not only in the builder that produces durable-nonce withdrawals. Chain cannot distinguish a compromised signing service from a leaked key.

  • Whether the hot-wallet key also leaked cannot be determined on chain.

  • Non-Solana chains are out of scope of this wiki entry (see the living report), except the five Relay BTC fills hashed above.
  • The Relay API record for the 398.4 SOL request was rate-limited; that link rests on the deposit-id / OP_RETURN match plus amount and timing.
  • No ownership or exchange label is known for the BTC recipient addresses; they are not on the FBI alert list.
